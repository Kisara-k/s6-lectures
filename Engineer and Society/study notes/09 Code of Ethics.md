## Study Notes

### 1. 📜 Introduction to Codes of Ethics in ICT

In the world of Information and Communication Technology (ICT), professionals often face complex decisions that can impact society, individuals, and organizations. To help guide ethical behavior, various **Codes of Ethics and Professional Conduct** have been developed. These codes are not just rules but serve as **recommended practices** that ICT professionals can consult when making decisions, especially in challenging situations. They also provide a framework for addressing violations and ensuring accountability.

These codes are created and maintained by professional bodies within the ICT industry, such as the **Association for Computing Machinery (ACM)**, **British Computer Society (BCS)**, **Australian Computer Society (ACS)**, and the **Computer Society of Sri Lanka (CSSL)**. In 2020, the **International Federation for Information Processing (IFIP)** adopted its own Code of Ethics, adapted from the ACM Code, to guide its member societies globally.


### 2. 🧭 The IFIP Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

The IFIP Code is structured into **four main parts**, each addressing different aspects of ethical behavior for computing professionals:

#### 2.1 General Ethical Principles

This section outlines the fundamental moral values that every computing professional should uphold:

- **Contribute to society and human well-being:** Recognize that computing affects all people and strive to make a positive impact.
- **Avoid harm:** Take care to prevent damage or injury to individuals or society.
- **Be honest and trustworthy:** Maintain integrity in all professional actions.
- **Be fair and take justice into account:** Treat all stakeholders equitably.
- **Respect intellectual property:** Acknowledge the effort behind new ideas and creations.
- **Respect privacy:** Protect personal information and the right to privacy.
- **Honor confidentiality:** Keep sensitive information secure and private.

#### 2.2 Professional Responsibilities

Here, the focus is on how professionals should conduct their work:

- **Strive for high quality:** Ensure that both the process and the final products meet high standards.
- **Maintain competence and ethical practice:** Continuously improve skills and behave ethically.
- **Know and respect rules:** Follow laws and organizational policies.
- **Accept professional review:** Be open to feedback and evaluation.
- **Analyze impacts and risks:** Consider the broader effects of computing systems.
- **Work within competence:** Only perform tasks you are qualified for.

#### 2.3 Professional Leadership Principles

Leaders in computing have additional responsibilities:

- **Prioritize public good:** Ensure that societal benefits are central to all work.
- **Promote social responsibility:** Encourage ethical behavior within teams.
- **Enhance work quality:** Manage resources and personnel to improve working conditions.
- **Support policies reflecting ethical principles:** Implement and uphold ethical guidelines.
- **Foster professional growth:** Create opportunities for learning and development.
- **Handle system changes carefully:** Be cautious when modifying or retiring systems, especially those critical to society.
- **Protect critical infrastructure:** Recognize the importance of systems integrated into societal functions.

#### 2.4 Compliance with the Code

Finally, professionals must:

- **Uphold and promote the Code:** Actively support ethical principles.
- **Address violations:** Treat breaches seriously and take appropriate action.


### 3. 💻 ACM Software Engineering Code of Ethics

The ACM Code is a detailed guide specifically for software engineers, emphasizing their unique ethical challenges. It is built around **eight key principles** that cover relationships and responsibilities:

#### 3.1 Concern for Public Interest

Software engineers must always prioritize the public good. This includes:

- Taking full responsibility for their work.
- Balancing competing interests fairly.
- Approving software only if it is safe.
- Disclosing any potential dangers.
- Cooperating to address public concerns.
- Being honest and avoiding deception.
- Considering accessibility issues.
- Volunteering skills for good causes.

#### 3.2 Client and Employer Responsibilities

Engineers should:

- Work only within their competence.
- Avoid using illegally obtained software.
- Use property only as authorized.
- Ensure documentation is approved.
- Respect confidentiality.
- Report problems promptly.
- Report social concerns.
- Refuse work that conflicts with their job.
- Prioritize client/employer interests unless moral concerns override.

#### 3.3 Product Standards

Engineers must aim for:

- High quality, reasonable cost, and realistic schedules.
- Clear and achievable goals.
- Addressing ethical, economic, cultural, legal, and environmental issues.
- Proper qualifications for work.
- Using appropriate methodologies and standards.
- Understanding and approving specifications.
- Accurate cost and schedule estimates.
- Adequate testing, debugging, and documentation.
- Respecting privacy in software and documentation.
- Using accurate and properly acquired data.
- Maintaining data integrity.
- Applying the same standards to maintenance as development.

#### 3.4 Judgment

Engineers should:

- Balance technical decisions with human values.
- Understand and agree with documents before endorsing.
- Remain objective.
- Avoid deceptive financial practices.
- Disclose conflicts of interest.
- Avoid decisions with conflicts of interest.

#### 3.5 Management

Managers must:

- Ensure good project management.
- Educate engineers on standards and confidentiality.
- Assign work based on abilities.
- Make reasonable estimates.
- Provide accurate information to employees.
- Pay fairly.
- Avoid unjust hiring practices.
- Negotiate fair intellectual property agreements.
- Provide due process for misconduct.
- Avoid asking employees to violate the Code.
- Protect employees who raise ethical concerns.

#### 3.6 Advancing the Profession

Software engineers should:

- Promote ethical conduct.
- Increase public knowledge of software engineering.
- Participate in professional activities.
- Support colleagues following the Code.
- Avoid self-interest over profession or clients.
- Obey laws unless public interest overrides.
- Avoid deception about software.
- Take responsibility for correcting errors.
- Show commitment to the Code.
- Avoid associations conflicting with the Code.
- Recognize violations as unprofessional.
- Report violations when necessary.

#### 3.7 Colleagues

Engineers should:

- Encourage adherence to the Code.
- Assist in professional development.
- Give credit fairly.
- Be objective in reviews.
- Give fair hearings.
- Help colleagues maintain good practices.
- Avoid unfair interference but protect public interest.
- Bring in experts when needed.

#### 3.8 Lifelong Learning

Professionals must:

- Stay current with developments.
- Improve software and documentation skills.
- Understand relevant standards and the Code.
- Avoid unfair treatment based on prejudice.
- Avoid encouraging Code violations.
- Recognize personal violations as unprofessional.


### 4. 🔒 Privacy and Confidentiality in ICT

#### 4.1 Understanding Privacy

Privacy is about controlling access to personal information and spaces. It can be physical (like your room or personal belongings) or informational (like your emails or relationships). Privacy is a **human right** because it supports:

- Personal growth and responsibility.
- Freedom to be oneself.
- Intellectual and spiritual development.
- Building trusting and intimate relationships.

However, privacy can also be misused to hide illegal or harmful activities, and sometimes it can prevent people from getting help.

#### 4.2 How Technology Affects Privacy

Modern technology makes it easier to collect, share, and combine information, which reduces privacy. We leave an **electronic trail** through our online activities, which can be accessed and used in many ways.

#### 4.3 Public Records and Privacy

Public records (like birth certificates, tax records, or criminal records) are held by government agencies and are often accessible online. While transparency is important, it can sometimes conflict with individual privacy rights. For example, releasing salary information of public employees can raise privacy concerns.

#### 4.4 Right to Information vs. Privacy

The **Right to Information (RTI)** laws require governments to disclose information upon request, promoting transparency. However, this must be balanced against privacy rights to avoid exposing sensitive personal data.

#### 4.5 Confidentiality

Confidentiality means protecting data once it is collected. This involves:

- Sharing data only on a need-to-know basis.
- Using proper security measures.
- Keeping data only as long as necessary.
- Using data only for the purpose it was collected.
- Anonymizing data when used for analysis to protect identities.


### 5. ⚖️ Legal Frameworks for Data Protection

Many countries have laws to protect personal data stored electronically. Examples include:

- **UK Data Protection Act 1998**
- **Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act No 9 of 2022**

Some sectors have specific regulations, such as banking or telecommunications.

#### 5.1 GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

The **GDPR** is a comprehensive data protection law in the European Union that came into effect in 2018. It aims to unify and strengthen data protection for individuals, giving them more control over their personal data and imposing strict rules on organizations handling such data.


### Summary

Ethical conduct in ICT is essential because technology deeply affects individuals and society. Codes of Ethics like those from IFIP and ACM provide detailed guidance on how professionals should behave, emphasizing honesty, fairness, competence, respect for privacy, and responsibility to the public. Privacy and confidentiality are critical concerns in the digital age, requiring careful handling of personal data and balancing transparency with individual rights. Legal frameworks like GDPR help enforce these principles, ensuring that data protection is taken seriously worldwide.
