## 3. Engineering Ethics 1

## Questions

#### 1. Which of the following best describe the attributes of a profession?  
A) Primarily focuses on maximizing individual financial gain  
B) Requires advanced formal education and intellectual judgment  
C) Has organizations controlled by members to set admission and conduct standards  
D) Involves routine tasks that can be easily mechanized  

#### 2. What distinguishes accountability from responsibility in a professional context?  
A) Responsibility involves accepting the outcomes of your actions  
B) Responsibility is about carrying out duties and following rules  
C) Accountability is owning the results of actions, including those of your team if you are a leader  
D) Accountability means performing assigned tasks diligently  

#### 3. Which of the following are considered core professional values?  
A) Accountability, responsibility, and professional attitude  
B) Personal ambition, secrecy, and competition  
C) Integrity, continuous learning, and teamwork  
D) Avoiding feedback, individualism, and risk-taking  

#### 4. Engineering ethics primarily focuses on which of the following?  
A) Setting standards for engineers’ obligations to the public, clients, employers, and the profession  
B) Ensuring engineers maximize profits for their employers  
C) Addressing ethical issues in both technical practice and business conduct  
D) Encouraging engineers to prioritize personal gain over public safety  

#### 5. Which of the following situations could present an ethical issue for an engineer?  
A) Sharing confidential proprietary information without permission  
B) Ensuring legal compliance by clients and contractors  
C) Refusing to update skills according to industry trends  
D) Accepting gifts or entertainment from a contractor that might influence decisions  

#### 6. Why is it important for engineers to study ethics?  
A) Because unethical engineering mistakes can cost lives, not just money  
B) To justify ignoring public safety concerns when under pressure  
C) To develop the willpower and judgment needed to handle ethical dilemmas  
D) To learn how to avoid all legal responsibilities  

#### 7. Which of the following statements about professional ethics codes are true?  
A) They only apply to engineers working in government roles  
B) They provide guidance on how to act toward people and institutions in a professional environment  
C) They are optional guidelines that professionals can choose to ignore without consequences  
D) They are often codified as a set of rules that all members of a profession must follow  

#### 8. What does “responsible leadership” in engineering ethics entail?  
A) Being aware of new technology and its impact on society  
B) Ignoring societal concerns to focus solely on technological advancement  
C) Using leadership to prioritize personal career advancement over ethical considerations  
D) Promoting engineering achievements while being truthful in professional statements  

#### 9. Which of the following best captures the relationship between ethics and engineering practice?  
A) Ethical principles guide engineers to provide safe and reliable goods and services  
B) Engineering ethics requires balancing technical constraints with ethical principles  
C) Engineers should always prioritize cost-saving over ethical concerns  
D) Ethics is a theoretical discipline unrelated to practical engineering decisions  

#### 10. In the context of professional ethics, which of the following is true about integrity?  
A) Integrity involves rejecting bribery and corrupt practices  
B) Being honest and non-deceptive is essential to build trust  
C) Taking credit for work done by others is acceptable if it benefits the company  
D) Integrity means avoiding responsibility for mistakes to protect one’s reputation

## Answers

#### 1. Which of the following best describe the attributes of a profession?  
A) ✗ Primarily focuses on maximizing individual financial gain — Professions focus on public good and ethical standards, not just personal profit.  
B) ✓ Requires advanced formal education and intellectual judgment — This is a key attribute of a profession.  
C) ✓ Has organizations controlled by members to set admission and conduct standards — Professional bodies regulate standards and membership.  
D) ✗ Involves routine tasks that can be easily mechanized — Professions involve non-routine, complex work, not mechanized tasks.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 2. What distinguishes accountability from responsibility in a professional context?  
A) ✗ Responsibility involves accepting the outcomes of your actions — This is accountability, not responsibility.  
B) ✓ Responsibility is about carrying out duties and following rules — Responsibility is about performing tasks properly.  
C) ✓ Accountability is owning the results of actions, including those of your team if you are a leader — Correct distinction of accountability.  
D) ✗ Accountability means performing assigned tasks diligently — This describes responsibility, not accountability.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 3. Which of the following are considered core professional values?  
A) ✓ Accountability, responsibility, and professional attitude — These are fundamental professional values.  
B) ✗ Personal ambition, secrecy, and competition — These are not professional values; secrecy and selfish ambition contradict ethics.  
C) ✓ Integrity, continuous learning, and teamwork — All are key professional values.  
D) ✗ Avoiding feedback, individualism, and risk-taking — Avoiding feedback and excessive individualism contradict professional attitude and teamwork.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 4. Engineering ethics primarily focuses on which of the following?  
A) ✓ Setting standards for engineers’ obligations to the public, clients, employers, and the profession — Core purpose of engineering ethics.  
B) ✗ Ensuring engineers maximize profits for their employers — Profit maximization is not the focus of ethics.  
C) ✓ Addressing ethical issues in both technical practice and business conduct — Ethics covers both technical and business aspects.  
D) ✗ Encouraging engineers to prioritize personal gain over public safety — This contradicts ethical principles.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 5. Which of the following situations could present an ethical issue for an engineer?  
A) ✓ Sharing confidential proprietary information without permission — Breaches confidentiality, an ethical violation.  
B) ✓ Ensuring legal compliance by clients and contractors — Ethical responsibility includes ensuring legal compliance.  
C) ✗ Refusing to update skills according to industry trends — While unprofessional, this is not necessarily an ethical violation.  
D) ✓ Accepting gifts or entertainment from a contractor that might influence decisions — This can lead to bribery or conflict of interest.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 6. Why is it important for engineers to study ethics?  
A) ✓ Because unethical engineering mistakes can cost lives, not just money — Safety and lives are at stake.  
B) ✗ To justify ignoring public safety concerns when under pressure — Ethics requires prioritizing safety, not ignoring it.  
C) ✓ To develop the willpower and judgment needed to handle ethical dilemmas — Ethics builds decision-making skills.  
D) ✗ To learn how to avoid all legal responsibilities — Ethics does not teach avoidance of legal duties.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 7. Which of the following statements about professional ethics codes are true?  
A) ✗ They only apply to engineers working in government roles — Ethics codes apply to all professionals regardless of employer.  
B) ✓ They provide guidance on how to act toward people and institutions in a professional environment — This is the purpose of ethics codes.  
C) ✗ They are optional guidelines that professionals can choose to ignore without consequences — Ignoring codes can lead to disciplinary action.  
D) ✓ They are often codified as a set of rules that all members of a profession must follow — Codes of conduct are formalized rules.  

**Correct:** B, D


#### 8. What does “responsible leadership” in engineering ethics entail?  
A) ✓ Being aware of new technology and its impact on society — Leaders must understand technology and social effects.  
B) ✗ Ignoring societal concerns to focus solely on technological advancement — Responsible leadership includes societal awareness.  
C) ✗ Using leadership to prioritize personal career advancement over ethical considerations — Ethics requires putting public good before personal gain.  
D) ✓ Promoting engineering achievements while being truthful in professional statements — Truthfulness is essential in leadership.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 9. Which of the following best captures the relationship between ethics and engineering practice?  
A) ✓ Ethical principles guide engineers to provide safe and reliable goods and services — Safety and reliability are ethical priorities.  
B) ✓ Engineering ethics requires balancing technical constraints with ethical principles — Engineers must consider both practical and ethical factors.  
C) ✗ Engineers should always prioritize cost-saving over ethical concerns — Ethics demands safety and integrity over cost-cutting.  
D) ✗ Ethics is a theoretical discipline unrelated to practical engineering decisions — Ethics is directly applied in engineering practice.  

**Correct:** A, B


#### 10. In the context of professional ethics, which of the following is true about integrity?  
A) ✓ Integrity involves rejecting bribery and corrupt practices — Avoiding corruption is fundamental to integrity.  
B) ✓ Being honest and non-deceptive is essential to build trust — Core aspect of integrity.  
C) ✗ Taking credit for work done by others is acceptable if it benefits the company — This violates honesty and integrity.  
D) ✗ Integrity means avoiding responsibility for mistakes to protect one’s reputation — Integrity requires owning mistakes, not avoiding them.  

**Correct:** A, B
