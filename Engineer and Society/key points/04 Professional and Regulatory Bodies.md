## 5. Professional and Regulatory Bodies

## Key Points

#### 1. 🎓 Profession and Professional Qualification  
- A profession requires advanced education and training (e.g., engineers, doctors, lawyers).  
- To become a professional, one must graduate in the specific area and gain skills through training and experience.  
- Degree standards vary: local university, foreign university conducted locally, or foreign university conducted abroad.  
- An independent body is needed to verify the standard of degrees.

#### 2. 🏢 Professional Bodies  
- Professional bodies are non-commercial, member-driven organizations governed by laws constituted by members.  
- They enroll members based on qualifications and experience and accredit education programs.  
- Main functions: maintain educational and industrial standards, support Continuing Professional Development (CPD), and govern ethical behavior.  
- CPD activities include training, seminars, conferences, webinars, and publications.  
- Membership categories: Student, Associate, Member, Senior Member, Fellow.

#### 3. 🌍 Examples of Professional Bodies  
- Sri Lanka: Institution of Engineers Sri Lanka (IESL), Institute of Chartered Accountants of Sri Lanka, Institute of Management Sri Lanka, Sri Lanka Institute of Marketing.  
- International: IET (UK), IMechE (UK), ICE (UK), IEEE (USA), Institute of Engineers Australia.

#### 4. 🛠️ Institution of Engineers Sri Lanka (IESL) Membership  
- Student Member: Undergraduate engineering students.  
- Affiliate Member: Holders of a 3-year engineering degree.  
- Associate Member: Holders of a 4-year engineering degree recognized by IESL.  
- Corporate Member (Chartered Engineer): Associate member + 5 years responsible experience, pass management and technical exams, submit reports.  
- Fellow: Chartered Engineer with superior experience and leadership roles.

#### 5. ⚖️ Regulatory Bodies  
- Regulatory bodies are public or government agencies established by law to regulate professions.  
- They set standards, impose requirements, and enforce compliance to protect public interest.  
- Regulatory bodies may be self-regulated or externally regulated.  
- Examples in Sri Lanka: Telecommunication Regulatory Commission, Consumer Affairs Authority, Public Utility Commission, Sri Lanka Medical Council, Engineering Council Sri Lanka (ECSL).

#### 6. 🏛️ Engineering Council Sri Lanka (ECSL)  
- ECSL regulates engineering practitioners, maintains professional standards and conduct, and registers engineers, diploma holders, and technicians.  
- ECSL council has 17 members: 4 ex-officio (including 3 university deans and TVEC Director General) and 13 nominated members from IESL, IIESL, Sri Lanka Engineering Service, and TVEC.  
- Practitioner categories: Chartered Engineer, Associate Engineer, Affiliate Engineer, Incorporated Engineer, Engineering Diplomate, Engineering Technician.

#### 7. 🎓 How to Become a Chartered Engineer (IESL)  
- Must be an Associate Member with a 4-year recognized degree.  
- Have 5 years of responsible experience under a Chartered Engineer.  
- Submit an experience report and a project/design report.  
- Pass the Management Paper (B Paper), Technical Paper (A Paper), and an interview.

#### 8. 📈 Engineering Career Path  
- Entry-level: Engineer (recruitment level).  
- Senior Engineer after ~5 years.  
- Deputy General Manager or Assistant Director after ~10 years (requires Chartered Engineer status).  
- Higher management roles (General Manager, Director, CEO) after 15-25 years depending on hierarchy type.
