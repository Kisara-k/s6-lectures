## 9. Professional Ethics

## Key Points

#### 1. ⚖️ Defensible vs Correct Decisions  
- Ethical dilemmas may have no correct decision, only defensible decisions.  
- A defensible decision is based on sound reasoning and can be defended in any forum.  
- The goal of ethical decision making is to make defensible decisions.

#### 2. 🧪 Informal Ethical Tests  
- Moral Intuition Test: First impression or reaction guides ethical judgment.  
- Shushers Test: Wanting to keep an action secret suggests it may be unethical.  
- Mom Test: If you wouldn’t want your mother to know about an action, it may be unethical.  
- TV Test: Willingness to disclose an action on TV indicates ethical acceptability.  
- Market Test: Actions should enhance personal or professional reputation.  
- Smell Test: If a situation “smells bad,” it is likely unethical.  
- Other Person’s Shoe Test: Consider the perspective of others affected by the action.

#### 3. 📚 Formal Ethical Theories  
- Consequentialist Theory focuses on outcomes; includes Hedonism, Egoism, and Utilitarianism.  
- Utilitarianism aims for the greatest happiness for the greatest number.  
- Deontologist Theory focuses on rights and duties of stakeholders.  
- Virtue Ethics asks what a virtuous person would do.  
- Kant’s Categorical Imperative (1st Formulation): Act only on maxims you want to become universal laws.  
- Kant’s Categorical Imperative (2nd Formulation): Treat people as ends, never merely as means.  
- The Golden Rule: Treat others as you want to be treated (positive and negative forms).

#### 4. 🛠️ Kallman and Grillo Four-Step Ethical Decision Process  
- Step 1: List and understand relevant facts, ethical issues, and stakeholders.  
- Step 2: Isolate the major ethical dilemma, phrased as a “should or should not” question.  
- Step 3: Analyze alternatives using:  
  - Consequentialism (harm and benefit analysis)  
  - Rights and Duties (identify violated rights and neglected duties)  
  - Kant’s Categorical Imperative (respect and universalizability)  
- Step 4: Make a defensible decision, justify it, plan implementation, and consider long-term prevention.

#### 5. 🌍 Santa Clara University Ethical Thinking Guide  
- Analyze if behavior promotes the greatest good or human welfare.  
- Identify legitimate rights and duties of affected individuals or groups.  
- Assess fairness in treatment of all parties.  
- Check consistency with recognized virtues.  
- Determine if the common good is served.
