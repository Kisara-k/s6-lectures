## 10. Code of Ethics

## Key Points

#### 1. 📜 Codes of Ethics in ICT  
- Codes of Ethics provide recommended practices to guide ethical conduct of ICT professionals.  
- They are prescriptive and offer guidance for decision-making and remediation of violations.  
- Professional bodies like ACM, BCS, ACS, CSSL provide these codes.  
- IFIP adopted its own Code of Ethics in 2020, adapted from the ACM Code.

#### 2. 🧭 IFIP Code of Ethics Structure  
- The IFIP Code consists of 4 parts: General Ethical Principles, Professional Responsibilities, Professional Leadership Principles, and Compliance with the Code.  
- General Ethical Principles include contributing to society, avoiding harm, honesty, fairness, respecting privacy and confidentiality.  
- Professional Responsibilities include striving for high quality, maintaining competence, respecting rules, accepting review, analyzing risks, and working within competence.  
- Professional Leadership Principles emphasize public good, social responsibility, quality of work life, policy support, professional growth, careful system modification, and protecting critical infrastructure.  
- Compliance requires upholding the Code and treating violations seriously.

#### 3. 💻 ACM Software Engineering Code of Ethics Key Principles  
- Concern for Public Interest: accept responsibility, balance interests, approve safe software, disclose dangers, avoid deception, volunteer skills.  
- Client and Employer: act within competence, avoid illegal software, respect confidentiality, report problems, prioritize client/employer interests unless moral concerns override.  
- Product Standards: aim for high quality, clear goals, address ethical/legal issues, use proper methodologies, ensure testing and documentation, respect privacy, maintain data integrity.  
- Judgment: support human values, remain objective, disclose conflicts of interest, avoid deceptive practices.  
- Management: ensure good project management, educate on standards, assign work fairly, pay fairly, provide due process, protect whistleblowers.  
- Advance Profession: promote ethics, participate professionally, obey laws, correct errors, avoid conflicts of interest, report violations.  
- Colleagues: encourage Code adherence, assist development, give credit, be objective, protect public interest, bring experts when needed.  
- Lifelong Learning: stay current, improve skills, understand standards and Code, avoid prejudice, discourage Code violations.

#### 4. 🔒 Privacy and Confidentiality  
- Privacy relates to control over access to personal information and spaces; it is a human right.  
- Benefits of privacy include personal growth, responsibility, freedom, and intimate relationships.  
- Technology erodes privacy by making data collection and sharing easier, leaving electronic trails.  
- Public records (e.g., birth certificates, tax records) are accessible but can conflict with privacy.  
- Right to Information laws require disclosure but must balance privacy concerns.  
- Confidentiality means keeping collected data secure, sharing only on a need-to-know basis, and using data only for its intended purpose.  
- Data should be anonymized when used for analysis.

#### 5. ⚖️ Legal Frameworks for Data Protection  
- Countries have laws protecting data, e.g., UK Data Protection Act 1998, Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act No 9 of 2022.  
- Sector-specific laws also exist (e.g., Banking Act, Telecom Act).  
- GDPR (EU Regulation 2016/679) strengthens and unifies data protection in the EU, effective from March 2018.
