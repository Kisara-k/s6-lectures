## 3. Engineering Ethics 1

## Questions

#### 1. Which of the following attributes are essential characteristics of a profession?  
A) Work requiring sophisticated skills and intellectual judgment  
B) Public recognition and enforcement of standards by professional bodies  
C) Membership requiring extensive formal education  
D) Routine tasks that can be mechanized easily  

#### 2. How do accountability and responsibility differ in a professional context?  
A) Accountability means taking ownership of the results of actions  
B) Accountability involves performing assigned duties diligently  
C) Responsibility refers to carrying out the tasks that lead to those results  
D) Responsibility is about accepting the outcomes of one’s actions  

#### 3. Which of the following best describe professional ethics?  
A) Codified rules that govern behavior within a professional group  
B) A set of personal values unique to each individual  
C) Laws enforced by government agencies  
D) Guidelines on how to act towards people and institutions in a business environment  

#### 4. What are some ethical issues engineers commonly face in their professional practice?  
A) Handling confidential or proprietary information appropriately  
B) Deciding project budgets without consulting stakeholders  
C) Conflict of interest and bribery  
D) Ensuring legal compliance by clients and contractors  

#### 5. Why is it critical for engineers to adhere to high ethical standards?  
A) Because unethical mistakes can lead to loss of money only  
B) Because ethical conduct is optional in technical decision-making  
C) Because engineers’ work impacts public safety and well-being  
D) Because professional behavior builds trust and credibility  

#### 6. Which of the following statements about professional values are true?  
A) Professional attitude includes being open to feedback and handling stress positively  
B) Teamwork requires ensuring all members are aligned and working together  
C) Continuous learning involves accepting what you don’t know and seeking knowledge  
D) Integrity means taking credit for others’ work if it benefits the company  

#### 7. What does “Respect for Law, Public Good, and Life” imply for engineers?  
A) Engineers must practice within legal boundaries and consider societal impact  
B) Engineers should ignore resource scarcity if it hinders project completion  
C) Engineers should prioritize company profits over public safety  
D) Engineers must focus solely on technical accuracy, regardless of public consequences  

#### 8. Which of the following are fundamental principles of engineering ethics?  
A) Accuracy and rigor in applying knowledge and skills  
B) Integrity and honesty in professional conduct  
C) Responsible leadership that promotes truthful communication  
D) Avoiding leadership roles to minimize ethical responsibility  

#### 9. In the context of engineering ethics, what is a potential risk of misusing ethical definitions?  
A) It encourages egoistic and non-cooperative behavior  
B) It ensures all engineers strictly follow the code of ethics  
C) It helps engineers avoid making difficult decisions  
D) It can lead to sabotage or misrepresentation of facts  

#### 10. Which of the following best describe the role of engineers as decision-makers in business environments?  
A) They are exempt from accountability for decisions made under pressure  
B) They face ethical dilemmas requiring knowledge and skills in ethics  
C) They must balance practical constraints with ethical principles  
D) They only focus on technical aspects without ethical considerations

## Answers

#### 1. Which of the following attributes are essential characteristics of a profession?  
A) ✓ Work requiring sophisticated skills and intellectual judgment is a key attribute of a profession.  
B) ✓ Public recognition and enforcement of standards by professional bodies is a defining feature.  
C) ✓ Membership requiring extensive formal education is essential for a profession.  
D) ✗ Routine tasks that can be mechanized are not characteristic of a profession.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 2. How do accountability and responsibility differ in a professional context?  
A) ✓ Accountability means taking ownership of the results of actions.  
B) ✗ Accountability is not about performing duties but about owning the outcomes.  
C) ✓ Responsibility refers to carrying out the tasks that lead to those results.  
D) ✗ Responsibility is about performing actions, not accepting outcomes.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 3. Which of the following best describe professional ethics?  
A) ✓ Professional ethics are codified rules governing behavior within a group.  
B) ✗ Professional ethics are not just personal values but shared codes.  
C) ✗ Professional ethics are not laws enforced by governments, but by professional bodies.  
D) ✓ They provide guidelines on how to act towards people and institutions in business.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 4. What are some ethical issues engineers commonly face in their professional practice?  
A) ✓ Handling confidential or proprietary information appropriately is an ethical issue.  
B) ✗ Deciding project budgets without consulting stakeholders is not specifically highlighted as an ethical issue here.  
C) ✓ Conflict of interest and bribery are common ethical issues.  
D) ✓ Ensuring legal compliance by clients and contractors is an ethical concern.  

**Correct:** A, C, D


#### 5. Why is it critical for engineers to adhere to high ethical standards?  
A) ✗ Mistakes can cost more than money, including lives.  
B) ✗ Ethical conduct is not optional; it is required in technical decision-making.  
C) ✓ Engineers’ work impacts public safety and well-being, making ethics critical.  
D) ✓ Professional behavior builds trust and credibility essential for the profession.  

**Correct:** C, D


#### 6. Which of the following statements about professional values are true?  
A) ✓ Professional attitude includes openness to feedback and stress management.  
B) ✓ Teamwork requires alignment and cooperation among members.  
C) ✓ Continuous learning involves accepting gaps in knowledge and seeking improvement.  
D) ✗ Integrity means being honest, not taking credit for others’ work.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 7. What does “Respect for Law, Public Good, and Life” imply for engineers?  
A) ✓ Engineers must work within legal boundaries and consider societal impact.  
B) ✗ Ignoring resource scarcity is unethical and unsustainable.  
C) ✗ Prioritizing profits over safety contradicts this principle.  
D) ✗ Focusing solely on technical accuracy without public concern is incomplete ethics.  

**Correct:** A


#### 8. Which of the following are fundamental principles of engineering ethics?  
A) ✓ Accuracy and rigor in applying knowledge and skills is fundamental.  
B) ✓ Integrity and honesty are core ethical principles.  
C) ✓ Responsible leadership includes truthful communication and societal awareness.  
D) ✗ Avoiding leadership roles is not ethical; leadership responsibility is emphasized.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 9. In the context of engineering ethics, what is a potential risk of misusing ethical definitions?  
A) ✓ It encourages egoistic and non-cooperative behavior.  
B) ✗ Misuse undermines adherence to the code of ethics rather than ensures it.  
C) ✗ Misuse does not help avoid decisions; it causes confusion.  
D) ✓ Misuse can lead to sabotage or misrepresentation of facts.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 10. Which of the following best describe the role of engineers as decision-makers in business environments?  
A) ✗ Engineers are accountable even under pressure; they are not exempt.  
B) ✓ Engineers face ethical dilemmas requiring ethical knowledge and skills.  
C) ✓ They must balance practical constraints with ethical principles.  
D) ✗ Engineers must consider ethics, not just technical aspects.  

**Correct:** B, C
