## Answers

#### 1. What best describes a defensible decision in professional ethics?  
A) ✓ A decision based on sound reasoning that can be justified in any forum — This matches the definition of a defensible decision.  
B) ✗ A decision that is always morally correct — Defensible decisions may not be perfectly correct but can be justified.  
C) ✗ A decision that aligns with personal intuition only — Personal intuition alone is insufficient for defensibility.  
D) ✗ A decision that avoids any negative consequences — Negative consequences may still exist in defensible decisions.  

**Correct:** A


#### 2. Which of the following informal ethical tests rely primarily on personal intuition or feelings?  
A) ✗ Kant’s Categorical Imperative — A formal ethical theory, not an informal test.  
B) ✓ Shushers Test — Relies on feeling uneasy about secrecy.  
C) ✓ Moral Intuition Test — Based on first impressions or gut feelings.  
D) ✓ TV Test — Relies on emotional reaction to public exposure.  

**Correct:** B, C, D


#### 3. The Shushers Test suggests an action is likely unethical if:  
A) ✓ You want to keep it secret from others — Wanting to hush an action implies unethical behavior.  
B) ✗ It can be publicly announced on TV — Opposite of secrecy.  
C) ✗ It benefits the majority — Benefit does not relate to secrecy.  
D) ✗ It respects everyone’s rights — Respecting rights usually means transparency.  

**Correct:** A


#### 4. Which of the following statements about the TV Test is true?  
A) ✗ It is a formal ethical theory — It is an informal test.  
B) ✗ It only applies to actions that are publicly broadcast — It is a hypothetical test, not limited to actual broadcasts.  
C) ✓ It helps assess whether you would be comfortable if your action was publicly known — This is the essence of the TV Test.  
D) ✗ It guarantees the ethicality of an action if you want to share it on TV — Wanting to share does not guarantee ethicality.  

**Correct:** C


#### 5. In consequentialist ethical theory, the right action is the one that:  
A) ✗ Respects individual rights above all else — Rights focus is deontological.  
B) ✓ Produces the greatest happiness for the greatest number — Core of utilitarian consequentialism.  
C) ✗ Maximizes personal benefit only — Egoism is a form of consequentialism but not the widely accepted ethical approach.  
D) ✗ Fulfills all duties regardless of outcome — This is deontological, not consequentialist.  

**Correct:** B


#### 6. Which ethical theory focuses on the rights and duties of stakeholders rather than consequences?  
A) ✗ Virtue Ethics — Focuses on character, not rights/duties.  
B) ✗ Utilitarianism — Consequentialist theory.  
C) ✓ Deontological Theory — Focuses on rights and duties.  
D) ✗ Egoism — Consequentialist, self-focused.  

**Correct:** C


#### 7. According to Kant’s First Formulation of the Categorical Imperative, an action is unethical if:  
A) ✗ It causes harm to no one — Lack of harm does not guarantee ethicality under this test.  
B) ✗ It treats people as ends in themselves — This is ethical, per Kant’s second formulation.  
C) ✓ It cannot be universalized without contradiction — This is the key test for ethical maxims.  
D) ✗ It benefits the majority — Benefit is not the focus here.  

**Correct:** C


#### 8. The Golden Rule in ethics can be summarized as:  
A) ✓ Treat others as you want to be treated — Positive form of the Golden Rule.  
B) ✗ Follow the law strictly — Law and ethics are related but not the same.  
C) ✗ Always maximize happiness — This is utilitarianism, not the Golden Rule.  
D) ✓ Do not treat others in ways you would not want to be treated — Negative form (Silver Rule).  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 9. Which of the following is NOT a step in the Kallman and Grillo Four-Step Process?  
A) ✗ Making a defensible decision and planning implementation — Step 4 of the process.  
B) ✓ Ignoring stakeholder interests to avoid bias — Stakeholders are central to the process, so ignoring them is incorrect.  
C) ✗ Listing and understanding the facts — Step 1 of the process.  
D) ✗ Isolating the major ethical dilemma — Step 2 of the process.  

**Correct:** B


#### 10. When analyzing an ethical dilemma using consequentialism, which question is NOT relevant?  
A) ✗ Which alternative results in the least harm? — Central to consequentialism.  
B) ✓ What rights are violated by the action? — Rights are deontological, not consequentialist.  
C) ✗ Who will be harmed if the action is done? — Relevant to harm assessment.  
D) ✗ Who benefits if the action is not done? — Relevant to benefit assessment.  

**Correct:** B


#### 11. In the Rights and Duties analysis, what is important to identify?  
A) ✗ The financial benefits of the action — Financials are not the focus here.  
B) ✓ Both the rights and corresponding duties of all stakeholders — Essential for this analysis.  
C) ✗ Only the rights of the decision-maker — All stakeholders’ rights matter.  
D) ✗ The popularity of the decision — Popularity is irrelevant to rights/duties.  

**Correct:** B


#### 12. Kant’s Second Formulation of the Categorical Imperative emphasizes:  
A) ✓ Treating both yourself and others as ends in themselves — Core of the second formulation.  
B) ✗ Maximizing overall happiness — Not Kantian, but utilitarian.  
C) ✗ Ignoring duties if consequences are positive — Kantian ethics prioritizes duties.  
D) ✗ Treating people as means to an end when necessary — This is unethical per Kant.  

**Correct:** A


#### 13. Which informal test would most directly challenge an action that “smells bad” or feels suspicious?  
A) ✓ Smell Test — Directly about sensing something wrong.  
B) ✗ Moral Intuition Test — More general gut feeling, not specifically “smell.”  
C) ✗ Mom Test — Focuses on whether you’d tell your mother.  
D) ✗ Market Test — Focuses on reputation, not intuition about smell.  

**Correct:** A


#### 14. Why might there be no “correct” decision in an ethical dilemma?  
A) ✓ Because outcomes always have both good and bad aspects — Ethical dilemmas often involve trade-offs.  
B) ✗ Because laws always dictate the correct choice — Laws may not cover all ethical issues.  
C) ✗ Because ethical theories always conflict — Conflicts exist but are not the only reason.  
D) ✗ Because all decisions are equally right — Not true; some are better justified.  

**Correct:** A


#### 15. The Market Test in informal ethical decision making focuses on:  
A) ✓ How the action affects one’s personal brand or reputation — Central to the Market Test.  
B) ✗ The financial cost of the action — Not the focus here.  
C) ✗ Whether the action can be advertised on TV — This is the TV Test.  
D) ✗ Whether the action is legal — Legality is separate from this test.  

**Correct:** A


#### 16. Which of the following best describes Virtue Ethics?  
A) ✗ It is based on universal moral laws — Kantian ethics.  
B) ✗ It focuses on the consequences of actions — This is consequentialism.  
C) ✗ It prioritizes maximizing happiness for the majority — Utilitarianism.  
D) ✓ It asks what a morally virtuous person would do in the situation — Core of virtue ethics.  

**Correct:** D


#### 17. In the Kallman and Grillo process, when making the final decision, what should be done if there are conflicting rights and duties?  
A) ✗ Avoid making a decision altogether — Avoidance is not a solution.  
B) ✗ Always prioritize the rights of the majority — Not always ethical or correct.  
C) ✓ Choose and defend the rights and duties that take precedence — Required for defensible decisions.  
D) ✗ Ignore the conflict and choose the easiest option — Not ethical.  

**Correct:** C


#### 18. Which of the following is a key question when applying Kant’s First Formulation to a proposed rule?  
A) ✗ Would everyone benefit financially if this rule were universal? — Financial benefit is not the test.  
B) ✗ Does this rule maximize happiness? — Utilitarian concern, not Kant’s.  
C) ✗ Does this rule respect my personal interests? — Kant’s test is universal, not personal.  
D) ✓ Can I will that everyone acts according to this rule without contradiction? — Central to Kant’s universalizability test.  

**Correct:** D


#### 19. According to the Santa Clara University ethical guide, fairness means:  
A) ✗ Avoiding any conflict of interest — Important but not the definition of fairness here.  
B) ✓ Treating parties equally or differently only for legitimate reasons — Fairness allows justified differences.  
C) ✗ Prioritizing the majority’s interests over minorities — Fairness requires balance.  
D) ✗ Treating everyone exactly the same regardless of context — Context matters.  

**Correct:** B


#### 20. Which of the following best describes the purpose of formal ethical theories in decision making?  
A) ✓ To provide persuasive, logical arguments supporting ethical choices — Helps justify decisions.  
B) ✗ To simplify ethical dilemmas by ignoring stakeholder interests — They require considering stakeholders.  
C) ✗ To replace personal judgment entirely — They guide but do not replace judgment.  
D) ✗ To guarantee a correct decision in every case — No guarantee of perfect decisions.  

**Correct:** A
