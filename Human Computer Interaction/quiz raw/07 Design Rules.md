## 7. Design Rules

## Questions

#### 1. Which of the following correctly describe the differences between principles, standards, and guidelines in design rules?

B) Standards have high authority and broad application  
C) Standards are always more general than principles

#### 2. Which principles support the learnability of a system?  
A) Familiarity and Consistency  
B) Observability and Recoverability  
C) Dialogue initiative and Multithreading  
D) Predictability and Synthesizability  

#### 3. Regarding flexibility in interaction design, which statements are true?  
A) Substitutivity allows equivalent input/output values to be interchanged  
B) Dialogue initiative means the system always controls the input dialogue  
C) Task migratability involves shifting task responsibility between user and system  
D) Customizability refers only to system-driven interface changes, not user-driven  

#### 4. Which of the following are principles of robustness in usability?  
A) Observability and Responsiveness  
B) Predictability and Familiarity  
C) Recoverability and Task conformance  
D) Multithreading and Dialogue initiative  

#### 5. Which statements about standards in design are accurate?  
A) Standards have low authority but high generality  
B) Standards require a sound theoretical basis and stable technology  
C) Software standards are more common and detailed than hardware standards  
D) ISO 9241 defines usability in terms of effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction  

#### 6. Which of the following best describe the role and characteristics of HCI design patterns?  
A) They represent invariant solutions to recurrent problems in specific contexts  
B) They are isolated and do not link to other patterns

B) Detailed guidelines (style guides) are more useful during later lifecycle stages  
C) Understanding the justification for guidelines helps resolve conflicts  
D) Guidelines are always mandatory and must be strictly followed  

#### 10. Which of the following pairs correctly match a principle with its description?  
A) Task conformance – degree to which system supports all user tasks  
B) Observability – ability to evaluate system state from its representation  
C) Predictability – assessing the effect of past actions  
D) Synthesizability – determining the effect of future actions

## Answers

#### 1. Which of the following correctly describe the differences between principles, standards, and guidelines in design rules?  
A) ✓ Guidelines have lower authority but more general application — Guidelines are less authoritative but more generally applicable than standards.  
B) ✗ Standards have high authority and broad application — Standards have high authority but limited (not broad) application.  
C) ✗ Standards are always more general than principles — Standards are more specific and less general than principles.  
D) ✓ Principles have high generality and low authority — Principles are abstract and broadly applicable but have lower authority.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 2. Which principles support the learnability of a system?  
A) ✓ Familiarity and Consistency — Both improve learnability by leveraging prior knowledge and uniform behavior.  
B) ✗ Observability and Recoverability — These are principles of robustness, not learnability.  
C) ✗ Dialogue initiative and Multithreading — These are principles of flexibility, not learnability.  
D) ✓ Predictability and Synthesizability — Both relate directly to learnability by helping users understand effects of actions.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 3. Regarding flexibility in interaction design, which statements are true?  
A) ✓ Substitutivity allows equivalent input/output values to be interchanged — This is the correct meaning of substitutivity.  
B) ✗ Dialogue initiative means the system always controls the input dialogue — Incorrect; dialogue initiative refers to freedom from system-imposed constraints, allowing user control.  
C) ✓ Task migratability involves shifting task responsibility between user and system — Correct definition of task migratability.  
D) ✗ Customizability refers only to system-driven interface changes, not user-driven — Incorrect; customizability includes both user-driven (adaptability) and system-driven (adaptivity) modifications.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 4. Which of the following are principles of robustness in usability?  
A) ✓ Observability and Responsiveness — Both are key robustness principles supporting system state awareness and communication speed.  
B) ✗ Predictability and Familiarity — These belong to learnability, not robustness.  
C) ✓ Recoverability and Task conformance — Both relate to robustness by supporting error correction and task support.  
D) ✗ Multithreading and Dialogue initiative — These are flexibility principles, not robustness.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 5. Which statements about standards in design are accurate?  
A) ✗ Standards have low authority but high generality — Standards have high authority but limited generality.  
B) ✓ Standards require a sound theoretical basis and stable technology — Standards depend on solid theory and slowly changing tech.  
C) ✗ Software standards are more common and detailed than hardware standards — Hardware standards are more common and detailed than software standards.  
D) ✓ ISO 9241 defines usability in terms of effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction — This is the correct definition from ISO 9241.  

**Correct:** B, D


#### 6. Which of the following best describe the role and characteristics of HCI design patterns?  
A) ✓ They represent invariant solutions to recurrent problems in specific contexts — This is the core definition of a pattern.  
B) ✗ They are isolated and do not link to other patterns — Patterns are linked in pattern languages to form complete designs.  
C) ✓ They embody values and facilitate communication among stakeholders — Patterns are intuitive and support communication.  
D) ✗ They capture design theory rather than practice — Patterns capture design practice, not just theory.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 7. Which of Shneiderman’s 8 Golden Rules emphasize error handling and user control?  
A) ✗ Enable frequent users to use shortcuts — Focuses on efficiency, not error handling or control.  
B) ✓ Offer error prevention and simple error handling — Directly addresses error management.  
C) ✓ Support internal locus of control — Encourages user control over the system.  
D) ✗ Reduce short-term memory load — Relates to cognitive load, not error handling or control.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 8. Norman’s principles include which of the following?  
A) ✓ Exploit the power of constraints, both natural and artificial — Correct Norman principle.  
B) ✓ When all else fails, standardize — Norman’s principle advocating standardization as a fallback.  
C) ✗ Design dialogs to yield closure — This is from Shneiderman’s rules, not Norman’s principles.  
D) ✓ Use both knowledge in the world and knowledge in the head — Fundamental Norman principle.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 9. Which of the following statements about guidelines are true?  
A) ✓ Abstract guidelines are more applicable during early lifecycle activities — Abstract guidelines guide early design phases.  
B) ✓ Detailed guidelines (style guides) are more useful during later lifecycle stages — Detailed guidelines help in implementation and refinement.  
C) ✓ Understanding the justification for guidelines helps resolve conflicts — Knowing rationale aids in decision-making.  
D) ✗ Guidelines are always mandatory and must be strictly followed — Guidelines are suggestive, not mandatory.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 10. Which of the following pairs correctly match a principle with its description?  
A) ✓ Task conformance – degree to which system supports all user tasks — Correct definition of task conformance.  
B) ✓ Observability – ability to evaluate system state from its representation — Correct definition of observability.  
C) ✗ Predictability – assessing the effect of past actions — Predictability relates to anticipating future actions, not past.  
D) ✗ Synthesizability – determining the effect of future actions — Synthesizability is about assessing past actions, not future.  

**Correct:** A, B
