## 0. Essence of HCI

## Key Points

#### 1. 🤝 Essence of HCI  
- HCI focuses on designing systems that are useful, usable, and used.  
- Useful means the system performs needed functions; usable means it is easy and enjoyable to use; used means it is accepted and integrated into real life.  
- Ubiquitous computing embeds sensors and devices everywhere, blurring the line between computer and environment.  
- Wearable computing blurs the line between computer and user.  
- HCI requires the ability to ask the right questions and answer them convincingly.  

#### 2. ⌨️ Gesture Keyboard (ShapeWriter)  
- Gesture keyboards allow users to input text by sliding fingers over letters, exploiting language redundancies.  
- Users can achieve speeds of 25-40 words per minute, with record speeds up to 99 wpm.  
- The technology was first released publicly by IBM in 2004 and later commercialized as ShapeWriter, Swype, and others.  
- Skill acquisition moves from slow, letter-by-letter tracing (closed-loop) to fast, whole-word gesturing (open-loop).  

#### 3. 🕰️ History of HCI  
- Ivan Sutherland’s Sketchpad (1963) introduced computer graphics and the first GUI concepts.  
- Douglas Engelbart invented the first mouse in the 1960s.  
- Xerox PARC developed the WIMP paradigm (Windows, Icons, Menus, Pointer) in the late 1970s.  
- Apple released the first commercial GUI computer, the Macintosh, in 1984.  
- Mark Weiser coined the term “Ubiquitous Computing” in 1988.  
- Mobile phones with GUIs became common in the late 1990s and 2000s, with the iPhone released in 2007.  

#### 4. 🧠 Experimental Psychology in HCI  
- HCI research uses controlled experiments with independent and dependent variables to test hypotheses.  
- Experiments must be reproducible and analyzed statistically.  
- There is a tradeoff between internal validity (control) and external validity (generalizability).  
- Low ecological validity means experimental results do not generalize well to real-world use.  

#### 5. 🔍 HCI Research Methods Beyond Experiments  
- Ethnographic methods study real-world use practices qualitatively.  
- Common qualitative methods include think-aloud protocols, structured interviews, and video coding.  
- Other methods include surveys, focus groups, participatory design, and automated log analysis.  

#### 6. 🌐 Current HCI Research Areas  
- Information visualization and surface computing.  
- Pervasive and ubiquitous computing.  
- Multiple, shared, and extended displays.  
- Privacy and social challenges in HCI.  
- Human-machine interfacing.  
- Crowdsourcing and human computational systems.  

#### 7. 💡 Principles and Foundations of HCI  
- HCI combines usability methods, system/tool creation, and modeling human behavior.  
- Psychological theories of perception, cognition, and motor control heavily influence HCI design.  
- Computer scientists need HCI literacy to build high-quality interactive systems.
