## 13. Future Trends in HCI

## Key Points

#### 1. 🖥️ Specialized Displays  
- Curved displays offer better viewing angles, less reflection, and improved 3D experience but are more expensive and harder to wall-mount.  
- UHD (4K) displays have approximately 4000 horizontal pixels, providing four times the resolution of HD.  
- Flexible displays use polyimide film as a backplane, allowing high curvature and flexibility.  
- Transparent displays with about 30% light transmittance have been developed for advertising and security applications.  

#### 2. ⌨️ Virtual and Projection Keyboards  
- Virtual keyboards project a keyboard layout onto flat surfaces and connect wirelessly (e.g., Bluetooth) to devices.  
- Examples include Celluon Epic and V200 key fob.  

#### 3. ✋ Gesture and Voice Recognition  
- Devices like smart TVs and gaming consoles recognize hand gestures (e.g., swipe, grab) and voice commands for control.  
- Universal remote devices can control multiple appliances using gestures and voice.  

#### 4. 🧠 Brain-Computer Interfaces  
- Brain signal capturing headsets use EEG electrodes to detect brain activity and convert it into digital signals.  
- Emotiv EPOC headset has 14 EEG channels and 2 references, used for controlling robotic arms, wheelchairs, and vehicles.  

#### 5. 🕶️ Augmented and Virtual Reality Devices  
- Microsoft HoloLens is an optical head-mounted display that blends 3D holograms with reality and runs Windows 10.  
- HTC Vive has 70 sensors and 360° head tracking with a 90 Hz refresh rate.  
- Oculus Rift offers low latency 360° head tracking and stereoscopic 3D views.  
- Google Cardboard is a low-cost VR headset made from cardboard that uses smartphones for display.  

#### 6. 👗 Virtual Mannequins and Fitting Rooms  
- Virtual mannequins are generated from user measurements to reduce clothing returns.  
- Virtual fitting rooms allow real-time virtual try-ons without physical changing rooms.  

#### 7. 🎮 Haptic Feedback Controllers  
- Devices like Steam Controller and Reactive Grip provide force and vibration feedback for gaming and medical applications.  

#### 8. ⌚ Wearable Technology  
- Google Glass is a smart glass HUD with camera, microphone, GPS, and voice commands (commercial production stopped in 2015).  
- Standalone smartwatches have integrated SIM slots for direct cellular connectivity without a paired phone.  
- Smart jewellery like CSR Bluetooth Pendant changes LED colors for notifications and mood; June Bracelet monitors UV exposure.  
- Smart shoes generate 3-4 mW power from walking using shock and swing harvesters; Bluetooth insoles monitor activity and provide navigation via vibrations.  
- Smart socks with embedded sensors provide running technique feedback via smartphone apps.  

#### 9. 🏃 Fitness and Sports Devices  
- Fitness trackers (e.g., Fitbit Charge HR, Jawbone UP Move) monitor heart rate, calories, and activities like running and swimming.  
- FitGuard mouth-guard contains accelerometers to detect impacts and uses LEDs to indicate injury severity.  
- M-Tracer golf swing analyzer attaches to clubs to record swing data and sends it to smartphones for analysis.  

#### 10. 🧬 Biomedical and Biometric Devices  
- Google’s smart contact lens measures glucose levels in tears and has a wireless chip and LED indicator.  
- Bionic contact lenses harvest RF and solar energy to power augmented reality displays on the lens.  
- Ultrasound fingerprint sensors can scan fingerprints through glass, metal, plastic, sweat, and lotions.  
- Bio-stamps are flexible, tattoo-like sensors that measure UV exposure, temperature, and blood pressure, powered wirelessly.  
- Closed loop insulin delivery systems use continuous glucose monitors and insulin pumps with control algorithms (PID, fuzzy logic) for autonomous insulin delivery.  
- Brain-controlled prosthetics use implanted sensors to read brain signals and control robotic limbs.  

#### 11. 🚗 Automobile and Racing Technologies  
- Modern cars support autonomous features like auto lane change and active cruise control with lane detection.  
- Tesla’s Autopilot mode enables semi-autonomous driving with emergency driver interaction.  
- Navdy’s transparent HUD projects navigation and messages on windshields, controlled by gestures and voice.  
- HUDWAY smartphone app turns phones into projection devices for windshield display without extra hardware.  
- D-air racing suit deploys airbags within 30 ms upon crash detection using accelerometers, gyroscopes, and GPS.  
- Ford’s heart attack detecting car seat uses ECG sensors and cameras to monitor driver health and can autonomously control the car in emergencies.  
- Safe driving apps like MotorMate use smartphone sensors to monitor driving behavior and provide feedback, potentially reducing insurance costs.  

#### 12. 🏠 Smart Home Devices  
- Nest thermostat detects human presence with motion sensors, learns user behavior, and controls heating/hot water.  
- Hive thermostat allows remote control and scheduling but does not learn user behavior.  
- Smart LED bulbs like STACK. bulbs have sensors to detect ambient light and occupancy, can turn off automatically, mimic daylight, and simulate presence for security.  

#### 13. 🌟 Miscellaneous HCI Innovations  
- Personal assistants (Apple Siri, Google Now, Microsoft Cortana) use natural language interfaces to perform tasks like calls, messaging, scheduling, and information retrieval.  
- Smart baby monitors (Sensible Baby SmartOne, Owlet Vitals) track baby’s position, movement, temperature, heart rate, and oxygen levels in real time via smartphone apps.  
- Google’s real-time natural language translation tool supports image-based translation across multiple languages.  
- Pavlok wristband helps break bad habits by delivering programmable mild electric shocks as reminders.
