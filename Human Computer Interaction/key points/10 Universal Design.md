## 10. Universal Design

## Key Points

#### 1. ♿ Universal Design Principles  
- Equitable use means design is useful to people with diverse abilities.  
- Flexibility in use accommodates a wide range of individual preferences and abilities.  
- Simple and intuitive use requires designs to be easy to understand regardless of experience or knowledge.  
- Perceptible information ensures communication of necessary info regardless of sensory abilities.  
- Tolerance for error minimizes hazards and consequences of unintended actions.  
- Low physical effort allows efficient and comfortable use with minimal fatigue.  
- Size and space for approach and use accommodate different body sizes and mobility.

#### 2. 👂 Multi-Sensory Systems  
- Multi-sensory systems use more than one sensory channel (e.g., sound, text, animation, vision).  
- Useful for users with special needs and virtual reality applications.  
- Computers typically use sight, sound, and sometimes touch; taste and smell are rarely used.

#### 3. 🎭 Multi-Modal vs Multi-Media  
- Multi-modal systems use more than one sense or mode of interaction (e.g., visual + auditory).  
- Multi-media systems use multiple media types but may use the same sensory mode (e.g., video, text, animation all visual).

#### 4. 🗣️ Speech Structure and Terminology  
- English has about 40 phonemes (basic sound units).  
- Allophones are context-dependent variations of phonemes (120-130 sounds).  
- Morphemes are the smallest meaningful units of language.  
- Prosody involves tone, stress, pitch, and pauses adding meaning.  
- Co-articulation is the influence of surrounding sounds on phoneme pronunciation.  
- Syntax is sentence structure; semantics is sentence meaning.

#### 5. 🖥️ Speech Recognition Challenges  
- Variations in accent, intonation, stress, and volume affect recognition.  
- Background noise interferes with accuracy.  
- Filler words like “um” and “err” complicate processing.  
- Understanding semantics and context is necessary for meaningful recognition.

#### 6. 🖥️ Speech Recognition Applications  
- Works best in limited vocabulary or single-user systems.  
- Useful when hands are occupied or for users with physical disabilities.  
- General wide-vocabulary speech recognition remains a challenge.

#### 7. 🗣️ Speech Synthesis  
- Generates artificial speech from text.  
- Prosody is difficult to replicate naturally.  
- Can be intrusive or hard to review.  
- Used in screen readers and spoken warnings.

#### 8. 🔊 Non-Speech Sounds  
- Used for warnings, alarms, and feedback (e.g., key clicks).  
- Language and culture independent.  
- Auditory icons use natural sounds mapped to actions (e.g., glass breaking for delete).  
- Earcons are synthetic structured sounds representing actions or objects.  
- Earcons can be combined into compound sounds and grouped into families.

#### 9. ✋ Touch and Haptic Feedback  
- Cutaneous perception involves skin sensations like vibration.  
- Kinesthetic feedback involves movement, force, and position sensing.  
- Technologies include electronic Braille displays and force feedback devices.

#### 10. ✍️ Handwriting Recognition  
- Captured via digitizing tablets or devices like PDAs.  
- Challenges include personal differences and co-articulation.  
- Some systems use special alphabets (e.g., Graffiti).  
- Usable but many users prefer keyboards.

#### 11. ✋ Gesture Recognition  
- Used for gestural input and sign language translation.  
- Technologies include data gloves and position sensors.  
- Benefits include natural interaction; challenges include variability and co-articulation.

#### 12. ♿ Assistive Technologies for Disabilities  
- Visual impairment: screen readers, auditory icons (e.g., SonicFinder).  
- Hearing impairment: text communication, captions, gesture.  
- Physical impairment: speech input/output, eye gaze, predictive keyboards.  
- Speech impairment: speech synthesis, text communication.  
- Dyslexia: speech input and output aids.  
- Autism: communication and educational tools.

#### 13. 🌍 Cultural and Demographic Considerations  
- Age affects interface needs (older adults need memory aids; children need age-appropriate devices).  
- Cultural factors influence interpretation of language, symbols, gestures, and colors.
