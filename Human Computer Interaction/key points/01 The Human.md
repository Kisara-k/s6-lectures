## 1. The Human

## Key Points

#### 1. 👁️ Vision  
- The retina contains rods for low light vision and cones for color vision.  
- Images are focused upside-down on the retina.  
- Visual angle indicates how much of the view an object occupies and relates to size and distance.  
- Visual acuity is the ability to perceive detail and improves with luminance.  
- Familiar objects are perceived as constant size despite changes in visual angle.  
- Blue color acuity is the lowest among colors.  
- About 8% of males and 1% of females are color blind.  
- The visual system compensates for movement and changes in luminance.  
- Optical illusions like the Ponzo and Müller-Lyer illusions occur due to overcompensation by the visual system.  
- Reading involves saccades and fixations; perception occurs during fixations.  
- Word shape and negative contrast improve reading from computer screens.

#### 2. 👂 Hearing  
- Humans hear frequencies from 20 Hz to 15 kHz.  
- Auditory system filters sounds to attend to specific sounds over background noise (cocktail party phenomenon).  
- Outer ear protects and amplifies sound; middle ear transmits vibrations; inner ear converts vibrations to nerve impulses.  
- Pitch corresponds to sound frequency; loudness corresponds to amplitude; timbre is the quality of sound.

#### 3. ✋ Touch  
- Skin receptors include thermoreceptors (heat/cold), nociceptors (pain), and mechanoreceptors (pressure).  
- Some body areas, like fingers, are more sensitive to touch than others.  
- Kinesthesia is awareness of body position and affects comfort and performance.

#### 4. 🏃 Movement  
- Reaction times: visual ~200 ms, auditory ~150 ms, pain ~700 ms.  
- Movement time depends on age, fitness, and stimulus type.  
- Fitts’ Law: $Mt = a + b \log_2(\frac{D}{S} + 1)$, where Mt = movement time, D = distance, S = target size.

#### 5. 🧠 Memory  
- Sensory memory types: iconic (visual), echoic (aural), haptic (tactile).  
- Short-term memory (STM) capacity is 7 ± 2 chunks; rapid access (~70 ms) and rapid decay (~200 ms).  
- Long-term memory (LTM) has huge capacity, slow access (~1/10 second), and slow decay.  
- LTM types: episodic (events) and semantic (facts, concepts, skills).  
- Semantic memory uses networks with inheritance for inference.  
- Frames organize information with fixed and variable slots.  
- Scripts represent stereotypical event sequences with roles, props, and scenes.  
- Production rules are condition-action pairs representing procedural knowledge.  
- Forgetting occurs via decay and interference (retroactive and proactive).  
- Recall requires reproduction of information; recognition is simpler and involves identifying familiar information.

#### 6. 🤔 Reasoning  
- Deductive reasoning derives logically necessary conclusions from premises but conclusions may be false if premises are false.  
- Inductive reasoning generalizes from observed cases but can only be disproved, not proven true.  
- Abductive reasoning infers the most likely cause from an effect but can lead to false conclusions.  
- Wason’s card task demonstrates difficulty humans have with negative evidence in reasoning.

#### 7. 🧩 Problem Solving  
- Problem space theory: problem solving involves generating states using legal operators within limits of human information processing.  
- Means-ends analysis is a heuristic used to select operators.  
- Analogical mapping uses knowledge from similar problems but is difficult if domains differ semantically.  
- Skilled problem solving involves chunking information to optimize STM.

#### 8. ⚠️ Errors  
- Slips: correct intention but incorrect execution, often due to inattention or poor skill.  
- Mistakes: incorrect intention caused by wrong understanding.  
- Mental models are internal representations of systems; errors occur if models are incorrect.

#### 9. ❤️ Emotion  
- James-Lange theory: emotion is interpretation of physiological response.  
- Cannon theory: emotion is psychological response to stimuli.  
- Schacter-Singer theory: emotion results from evaluating physiological responses in context.  
- Positive affect improves creative problem solving; negative affect narrows thinking and impairs even easy tasks.  
- Stress increases difficulty of problem solving; relaxed users are more forgiving of design flaws.

#### 10. 👥 Individual Differences  
- Long-term differences include sex, physical and intellectual abilities.  
- Short-term differences include stress and fatigue.  
- Age affects capabilities and changes over time.  
- Design decisions should consider these differences to avoid excluding users.
