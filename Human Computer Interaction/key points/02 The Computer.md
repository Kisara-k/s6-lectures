## 2. The Computer

## Key Points

#### 1. ⌨️ Keyboard Layouts and Text Entry  
- QWERTY layout was designed to prevent typewriter jams, not for typing speed.  
- Dvorak layout improves typing speed by 10-15% and reduces fatigue but is not widely adopted due to social resistance.  
- Alphabetic keyboard layouts are not faster for beginners or trained typists.  
- Chord keyboards use combinations of few keys to type letters and are compact but have limited adoption.  
- T9 predictive text allows typing on numeric phone pads by guessing words from key sequences.

#### 2. 🖱️ Pointing Devices  
- The mouse detects relative movement on a flat surface and moves the cursor in the (x, y) plane.  
- Mechanical mice use a rolling ball and potentiometers; optical mice use LEDs and reflected light intensity.  
- Touchpads are small touch-sensitive tablets mainly used in laptops, with adjustable acceleration for speed and precision.  
- Trackballs rotate a ball inside a static housing to move the cursor; thumbwheels provide precise control for CAD or scrolling.  
- Joysticks control cursor velocity via stick pressure and have buttons for selection.  
- Touchscreens detect finger or stylus contact by interrupting light beams, capacitance changes, or ultrasonic reflections.

#### 3. 🖥️ Display Technologies  
- Bitmap screens consist of pixels; resolution is measured in pixels (width x height) and pixel density in dpi (dots per inch).  
- Common aspect ratios are 4:3 for most screens and 16:9 for widescreen TVs.  
- Color depth varies from black/white to 24-bit color (millions of colors).  
- CRT displays use electron beams to excite phosphor-coated screens.  
- LCDs use polarized light and crystals to control color and are lighter and radiation-free compared to CRTs.  
- Digital paper uses tiny spheres or liquid channels to display images electronically and retain them without power.

#### 4. 🕶️ Virtual Reality and 3D Interaction  
- VR headsets provide separate screens for each eye with slightly different angles to create a 3D effect.  
- Motion sickness in VR is caused by lag between head movement and display update and conflicting depth cues.  
- 3D mice allow six degrees of freedom: x, y, z + roll, pitch, yaw.  
- Data gloves use fiber optics to detect finger positions for 3D interaction.

#### 5. 🔊 Physical Interaction and Bio-sensing  
- Haptic devices provide force feedback and vibration, important in gaming and simulations.  
- Bio-sensors can monitor heart rate, skin response, iris patterns, and other body data for adaptive interfaces.  
- Environmental sensors include ultrasound detectors, RFID tags, temperature, and weight sensors.

#### 6. 🖨️ Printing and Scanning  
- Dot-matrix printers use pins to strike an inked ribbon, with typical resolution of 80-120 dpi.  
- Ink-jet printers spray tiny ink droplets, typically achieving 300 dpi or better.  
- Laser printers use electrostatic charge and toner, typically achieving 600 dpi or better.  
- Fonts are measured in points (1 pt ≈ 1/72 inch) and can be fixed-pitch (e.g., Courier) or variable-pitch (e.g., Times Roman).  
- Page description languages like PostScript describe complex pages for printing instead of sending bitmaps.  
- Scanners convert paper into bitmaps; flat-bed scanners digitize whole pages, hand-held scanners digitize strips.  
- Optical Character Recognition (OCR) converts scanned images into editable text but struggles with varied fonts and layouts.

#### 7. 💾 Storage and Compression  
- Lossless compression (e.g., ZIP, GIF) allows exact recovery of original data.  
- Lossy compression (e.g., JPEG, MP3) approximates original data by exploiting human perception.  
- Text storage formats include ASCII (7-bit), UTF-8 (8-bit), RTF (formatted text), SGML/XML (structured documents).  
- Image formats include JPEG, TIFF, GIF; audio/video formats include MPEG, WAV, QuickTime.

#### 8. ⚡ Processing Speed and Performance  
- Moore’s Law states processor speed doubles approximately every 18 months; memory capacity doubles every 12 months.  
- Common performance bottlenecks include computation speed, storage data transfer, graphics updating, and network capacity.  
- Too slow processing causes lag and user frustration; too fast processing can cause issues like unreadable fast-scrolling text.
