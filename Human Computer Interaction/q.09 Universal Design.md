## Answers

#### 1. Which of the following are principles of Universal Design?  
A) ✓ Equitable use is a core principle ensuring accessibility for all.  
B) ✗ High physical effort contradicts the principle of low physical effort.  
C) ✓ Simple and intuitive use is essential for usability by diverse users.  
D) ✓ Size and space for approach and use ensures accessibility regardless of body size or mobility.  

**Correct:** A, C, D


#### 2. Multi-modal systems differ from multi-media systems because they:  
A) ✓ Multi-modal systems use more than one sensory mode (e.g., visual and auditory).  
B) ✓ Multi-media systems use multiple media types but often within the same sensory mode (e.g., video and text both visual).  
C) ✗ Multi-modal systems are not limited to video and animation; they include any sensory modes.  
D) ✗ Multi-modal systems are not limited to visual and auditory only; theoretically, other senses could be included.  

**Correct:** A, B


#### 3. Which senses are currently most practical to use in computer interfaces?  
A) ✓ Sight and sound are widely used and practical.  
B) ✗ Taste and smell are rarely used due to technical and practical limitations.  
C) ✓ Touch is sometimes used, especially with haptic devices.  
D) ✗ Smell and taste are not practical in current mainstream interfaces.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 4. Phonemes are best described as:  
A) ✗ Morphemes are the smallest meaningful units, not phonemes.  
B) ✓ Phonemes are the basic atomic units of sound in speech.  
C) ✗ Allophones are variations of phonemes, not phonemes themselves.  
D) ✗ Syntax refers to sentence structure, not phonemes.  

**Correct:** B


#### 5. Prosody in speech refers to:  
A) ✗ Semantics deals with meaning, not prosody.  
B) ✓ Prosody involves rhythm, stress, and intonation, adding meaning beyond words.  
C) ✗ Phonemes are smallest sound units, not prosody.  
D) ✗ Syntax is sentence structure, unrelated to prosody.  

**Correct:** B


#### 6. The Phonetic Typewriter system:  
A) ✗ Performs worse on untrained speakers; it generalizes poorly.  
B) ✓ Uses neural networks to cluster similar sounds for recognition.  
C) ✓ Requires a large dictionary to correct minor variations in speech.  
D) ✗ Designed for Finnish, a phonetic language, not complex spelling languages.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 7. Speech recognition systems are most effective when:  
A) ✗ Open vocabulary, general use systems still face significant challenges.  
B) ✓ Limited vocabulary or single-user systems perform better.  
C) ✓ Useful when users’ hands are occupied, e.g., driving.  
D) ✗ Training on multiple speakers improves performance but is still challenging.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 8. Which of the following are challenges of speech synthesis?  
A) ✓ Producing natural prosody is difficult.  
B) ✓ Speech synthesis can be intrusive in shared environments.  
C) ✗ It is transient and harder to review, not easier to browse.  
D) ✓ Speech synthesis can create noise disturbing others.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 9. Non-speech sounds are useful because they:  
A) ✓ They are language and culture independent, making them widely understandable.  
B) ✗ They cannot replace speech in all communication contexts.  
C) ✓ Provide immediate feedback, especially for transient information.  
D) ✗ Not always easier to interpret than speech; context matters.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 10. Auditory icons differ from earcons in that auditory icons:  
A) ✓ Use natural sounds with intuitive meanings.  
B) ✗ Earcons are synthetic structured sounds, not natural.  
C) ✗ Earcons, not auditory icons, are combined to represent complex information.  
D) ✗ Auditory icons have more natural mappings, so this is false.  

**Correct:** A


#### 11. Which of the following are true about earcons?  
A) ✓ Earcons are structured combinations of notes representing actions or objects.  
B) ✗ Earcons often lack natural mappings, making them harder to associate with tasks.  
C) ✓ Family earcons group similar sounds for related actions or objects.  
D) ✗ Earcons are generally harder to learn than auditory icons due to lack of natural meaning.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 12. Haptic interaction includes:  
A) ✓ Cutaneous perception involves skin sensations like vibrations.  
B) ✓ Kinesthetic feedback relates to movement and force.  
C) ✗ Visual feedback is not part of haptic interaction.  
D) ✓ Temperature and texture are part of tactile information.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 13. Handwriting recognition technology faces challenges because:  
A) ✗ Users write letters differently, causing variability.  
B) ✓ Co-articulation causes letters to blend, complicating recognition.  
C) ✓ Stroke data is captured as sequences of dots for processing.  
D) ✗ Large tablets are used but smaller devices are also common; not limited to large tablets.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 14. Gesture recognition benefits include:  
A) ✓ Gestures provide a natural form of interaction.  
B) ✗ Gesture recognition is user-dependent and variable, not completely independent.  
C) ✓ Helps communication between signing and non-signing users.  
D) ✗ Variability and co-articulation issues remain challenges.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 15. Assistive technologies for users with physical impairments may include:  
A) ✓ Speech input/output can assist those with limited mobility.  
B) ✓ Eye-gaze tracking allows hands-free control.  
C) ✓ Predictive keyboards help reduce physical effort.  
D) ✗ Auditory icons alone do not address physical impairments specifically.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 16. Which of the following are considerations when designing for cultural differences?  
A) ✓ Language and symbol interpretation varies culturally.  
B) ✓ Color and gesture meanings differ across cultures.  
C) ✗ Ignoring political or religious influences risks alienating users.  
D) ✓ Gender and generation affect interface acceptability.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 17. Dual mode displays are effective because they:  
A) ✓ Present information redundantly through two sensory channels.  
B) ✗ Using only visual information limits accessibility and redundancy.  
C) ✓ Help resolve ambiguity in one mode through another.  
D) ✗ Useful for all users, not only those with hearing impairments.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 18. Which of the following statements about multi-media systems is true?  
A) ✗ Multi-media systems do not always use multiple sensory modes; often they use multiple media within one mode.  
B) ✓ They may combine video, animation, text, and images within the visual mode.  
C) ✗ They often use sound as a medium, including speech and non-speech sounds.  
D) ✗ Multi-media systems are used in many applications beyond education.  

**Correct:** B


#### 19. The main limitation of current computer systems in using all five human senses is:  
A) ✓ Difficulty integrating taste and smell into interfaces limits full sensory use.  
B) ✗ There is interest in multi-sensory interaction, but technical challenges remain.  
C) ✗ Over-reliance on touch is not the main limitation.  
D) ✗ Sight and sound are effectively used already.  

**Correct:** A


#### 20. Which of the following are true about speech recognition and synthesis in assistive technology?  
A) ✓ Speech recognition is useful when users’ hands are occupied.  
B) ✗ Speech synthesis is not always preferred over text; some users prefer text for reviewability.  
C) ✓ Both face challenges with prosody and naturalness.  
D) ✓ Speech synthesis can be intrusive in shared environments.  

**Correct:** A, C, D
