## 5. Marks and Channels

## Key Points

#### 1. 🎨 Marks and Channels Definitions  
- Marks are geometric primitives representing data items or links.  
- Channels control the appearance of marks based on data attributes.  
- Marks and channels together form the basis of visual encoding.

#### 2. 🔷 Types and Constraints of Marks  
- Points have no fixed size constraints and can encode multiple attributes via size and shape.  
- Lines have one size constraint (length) but can encode other attributes via width or color.  
- Areas have two size constraints (length and width) and cannot encode additional attributes by size or shape.  
- 3D volume marks are rarely used.

#### 3. 🌈 Channels and Their Properties  
- Channels include position, color (hue, saturation, brightness), size, shape, orientation, and texture.  
- Channels differ in the type and amount of information they can convey.  
- Redundant encoding uses multiple channels for the same attribute to strengthen the message.

#### 4. 📊 Expressiveness and Effectiveness of Channels  
- Expressiveness: matching channel type to data type (quantitative, ordinal, categorical).  
- Effectiveness: how accurately humans perceive differences in a channel.  
- Position and length are among the most effective channels for quantitative data.

#### 5. 🎯 Channel Effectiveness Criteria  
- Accuracy: precision in distinguishing encoded values (length is highly accurate).  
- Discriminability: number of distinct perceptible steps in a channel (line width supports few bins).  
- Separability: ability to perceive one channel independently of others.  
- Popout: some channels allow immediate detection of distinct items (e.g., color hue).

#### 6. 🔍 Factors Affecting Perceptual Accuracy  
- Alignment on a common scale improves accuracy.  
- Presence of distractors reduces accuracy.  
- Distance between marks affects perception.  
- Relative judgments dominate over absolute judgments (Weber’s Law).  

#### 7. 🌟 Color and Luminance Perception  
- Luminance perception is contextual and depends on contrast with surroundings.  
- Color constancy allows stable color perception under varying illumination.
