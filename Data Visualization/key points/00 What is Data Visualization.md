## 1. What is Data Visualization

## Key Points

#### 1. 📊 Definition of Data Visualization  
- Data visualization provides visual representations of datasets to help people carry out tasks more effectively.  
- Visualization is used to augment human capabilities, not replace human decision-making with automatic methods.

#### 2. 🧠 Human in the Loop  
- Visualization is necessary when fully automatic solutions do not exist or are not fully trusted.  
- Many analysis problems are ill-specified, meaning the exact questions to ask are not known in advance.  
- Visualization supports exploratory analysis, presentation of known results, requirement assessment, refinement of automatic solutions, and verification by end users.

#### 3. 👁️ Why Vision is Used  
- The human visual system is a high-bandwidth channel to the brain, allowing overview through background processing.  
- Visual processing occurs in parallel and pre-attentively, enabling simultaneous perception of many elements.  
- Other senses like sound, touch, taste, and smell have lower bandwidth or no viable record/replay devices for data visualization.

#### 4. 🔍 Representing All Data  
- Summaries lose information; details matter for confirming expectations and finding unexpected patterns.  
- Anscombe’s Quartet demonstrates that datasets with identical statistics can look very different visually, highlighting the importance of visualizing raw data.

#### 5. ⚙️ Resource Limitations  
- Visualization design must consider computational limits (time and memory), display limits (pixels as a constrained resource), and human limits (time, memory, attention).  
- There is a tradeoff between clutter (too dense) and wasted space (too sparse) in visualizations.  
- Information density is the ratio of space used to encode information versus unused whitespace.

#### 6. 🧩 Importance of Analysis in Visualization  
- Analysis imposes structure on the large design space of visualization, helping designers think systematically.  
- Analyzing existing visualizations serves as a stepping stone to designing new ones.  
- Most visualization possibilities are ineffective for particular task/data combinations, so analysis helps identify effective designs.
