## 6. Perception

## Key Points

#### 1. ⚡ Fast and Slow Thinking  
- System 1 is fast, unconscious, automatic, and makes 98% of all our thinking.  
- System 1 operates without self-awareness or control and handles tasks like reading faces.  
- System 2 is slow, conscious, deliberate, effortful, and makes 2% of all our thinking.  
- System 2 involves logical, skeptical thinking with self-awareness and controls complex tasks like solving math problems.

#### 2. 👀 Human Vision  
- The eyes sample the environment 3-4 times per second to build a mental picture.  
- Visual perception depends on what is actually there, what we expect to see, and where attention is directed.  
- Visual working memory capacity is limited to 3-4 simple shapes.

#### 3. 🎯 Selective Attention  
- We perceive only what the mind attends to.  
- The Basketball Pass Test demonstrates how focused attention causes people to miss unexpected events.  
- The Door Study shows people often fail to notice when the person they are talking to is replaced.  
- The Multi-Colour Card Trick illustrates how attention can be misdirected, causing missed changes.

#### 4. 🌈 Color Perception  
- Color perception is influenced by the presence of shadows.  
- Color perception is influenced by the color of light sources.

#### 5. 🏞️ Depth Perception  
- Depth perception relies on visual cues like relative size, shadows, and motion to interpret 3D space.

#### 6. 🧠 Visual Information Processing (Stephen Few)  
- Visual perception is selective; we are sensitive to contrast and change.  
- We tend to see what we know or expect (familiar objects attract attention).  
- Short-term visual memory has a small capacity, limiting information perceived at once.

#### 7. ⚡ Pre-attentive vs. Post-attentive Perception  
- Pre-attentive perception is immediate, automatic, and primal.  
- Post-attentive perception is slower, conscious, and requires mental effort to interpret patterns.

#### 8. 🧩 Gestalt Principles  
- Similarity: elements with shared visual properties are grouped together.  
- Proximity: elements close to each other are perceived as related.  
- Enclosure: surrounding elements with a boundary groups them visually.  
- Common Fate: shapes moving in the same direction and speed are grouped.  
- Parallelism: lines with similar slopes are visually associated.  
- Connectedness: elements connected by lines or shapes are perceived as related.

#### 9. 🎨 Pre-attentive Visual Attributes  
- Color intensity and hue are noticed instantly.  
- Line width and length variations stand out immediately.  
- Spatial position and orientation attract quick attention.  
- Enclosure, size, shape, and curvature are pre-attentive features.  
- 3D depth, focus and blur, and conjunctions of shape and color are also pre-attentive attributes.
