## 1. What is Data Visualization

## Questions

#### 1. What are the main reasons for involving a human in the data visualization process?  
A) To replace computational decision-making entirely  
B) To help verify and build trust in automatic solutions  
C) Because many analysis problems are ill-specified and require exploratory analysis  
D) To ensure all data is summarized before analysis

A) Vision provides a high-bandwidth channel allowing parallel and pre-attentive processing  
B) Sound offers a higher bandwidth but is less intuitive for data overview  
C) Vision processes information sequentially, which is better for detailed analysis  
D) Touch has limited record and replay capacity, making it low bandwidth  

#### 3. Which of the following statements about external representations in visualization are true?  
A) They are only useful when the data is fully summarized  
B) They help people carry out tasks more effectively by providing visual forms of data  
C) They replace human cognition with perception to reduce mental effort  
D) They always eliminate the need for human analysis  

#### 4. What challenges arise from display limitations when designing visualizations?  
A) Designers must find a sweet spot between dense and sparse layouts  
B) Display limitations are negligible compared to computational limits  
C) Increasing information density always improves understanding  
D) Pixels are a limited resource, requiring a balance between clutter and wasted space  

#### 5. Why is it important to visualize all the data rather than just summary statistics?  
A) Identical statistics can correspond to very different data distributions  
B) Summary statistics provide a complete understanding of the dataset  
C) Visualizing all data helps confirm the validity of statistical models  
D) Summary statistics can hide important details and unexpected patterns  

#### 6. Which of the following are true about the human limits that affect visualization design?  
A) Human limits are less important than computational limits in visualization design  
B) Human time and attention are limited, so visualizations must avoid overload  
C) Humans have unlimited attention and memory for analyzing visualizations  
D) Visualizations should be designed to respect human cognitive constraints  

#### 7. In what ways can visualization support the development and refinement of automatic data analysis solutions?  
A) By providing a stepping stone to assess requirements before model development  
B) By helping developers tune parameters through visual inspection  
C) By allowing end users to verify and build trust in automatic outputs  
D) By fully automating the analysis process without human input  

#### 8. Which of the following best describe the role of analysis in visualization design?  
A) It is unnecessary if the visualization is created by an expert  
B) It helps designers learn from existing visualizations to improve new designs  
C) It imposes structure on the large design space to help systematic thinking  
D) It limits creativity by enforcing strict rules on visualization choices  

#### 9. Considering resource limitations, which of the following statements are accurate?  
A) Computational limits include system memory and processing time constraints  
B) Designers should prioritize computational limits over human and display limits  
C) Human limits include time, memory, and attention constraints  
D) Display limits are irrelevant if the dataset is small  

#### 10. Regarding the example of Anscombe’s Quartet, what does it illustrate about data visualization?  
A) Visualizing raw data is crucial to detect anomalies and validate models  
B) Summary statistics are sufficient to understand all aspects of a dataset  
C) Different datasets can have identical summary statistics but very different visual patterns  
D) Visualization is unnecessary if statistical measures are available

## Answers

#### 1. What are the main reasons for involving a human in the data visualization process?  
A) ✗ Humans are not meant to replace computational decision-making entirely; visualization supports human judgment.  
B) ✓ Visualization helps verify and build trust in automatic solutions.  
C) ✓ Many analysis problems are ill-specified, requiring exploratory analysis by humans.  
D) ✗ Summarizing all data before analysis is not always possible or desirable; details often matter.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 2. Why is vision considered the most effective sense for data visualization compared to sound or touch?  
A) ✓ Vision provides a high-bandwidth channel with parallel and pre-attentive processing.  
B) ✗ Sound has lower bandwidth and is sequential, making it less effective for overview.  
C) ✗ Vision processes information simultaneously, not sequentially, which is an advantage.  
D) ✓ Touch has limited record/replay capacity, making it low bandwidth for data.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 3. Which of the following statements about external representations in visualization are true?  
A) ✗ They are useful beyond summarized data, often representing raw or detailed data.  
B) ✓ They help people carry out tasks more effectively by providing visual forms of data.  
C) ✓ External representations replace some cognition with perception, reducing mental effort.  
D) ✗ They do not eliminate the need for human analysis; humans remain essential.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 4. What challenges arise from display limitations when designing visualizations?  
A) ✓ Designers must find a balance ("sweet spot") between dense and sparse layouts.  
B) ✗ Display limitations are significant and cannot be ignored compared to computational limits.  
C) ✗ Increasing information density beyond a point causes clutter and confusion.  
D) ✓ Pixels are limited, so balancing clutter and wasted space is necessary.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 5. Why is it important to visualize all the data rather than just summary statistics?  
A) ✓ Identical statistics can correspond to very different data distributions (e.g., Anscombe’s Quartet).  
B) ✗ Summary statistics alone do not provide a complete understanding of the dataset.  
C) ✓ Visualizing all data helps confirm the validity of statistical models.  
D) ✓ Summaries can hide important details and unexpected patterns.  

**Correct:** A, C, D


#### 6. Which of the following are true about the human limits that affect visualization design?  
A) ✗ Human limits are as important as computational and display limits in design.  
B) ✓ Human time and attention are limited, so overload must be avoided.  
C) ✗ Humans have limited attention and memory, not unlimited.  
D) ✓ Visualizations should respect human cognitive constraints.  

**Correct:** B, D


#### 7. In what ways can visualization support the development and refinement of automatic data analysis solutions?  
A) ✓ Visualization helps assess requirements before model development.  
B) ✓ Developers can tune parameters by visually inspecting outputs.  
C) ✓ Visualization allows end users to verify and build trust in automatic outputs.  
D) ✗ Visualization does not fully automate analysis; human input remains necessary.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 8. Which of the following best describe the role of analysis in visualization design?  
A) ✗ Analysis is necessary regardless of expertise to avoid ineffective designs.  
B) ✓ Learning from existing visualizations improves new designs.  
C) ✓ Analysis imposes structure on the large design space for systematic thinking.  
D) ✗ Analysis guides design choices but does not limit creativity strictly.  

**Correct:** B, C


#### 9. Considering resource limitations, which of the following statements are accurate?  
A) ✓ Computational limits include memory and processing time constraints.  
B) ✗ Designers must balance all limits; prioritizing one over others is not always correct.  
C) ✓ Human limits include time, memory, and attention constraints.  
D) ✗ Display limits matter even for small datasets due to pixel and space constraints.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 10. Regarding the example of Anscombe’s Quartet, what does it illustrate about data visualization?  
A) ✓ Visualizing raw data is crucial to detect anomalies and validate models.  
B) ✗ Summary statistics alone are insufficient to understand all aspects of data.  
C) ✓ Different datasets can have identical summary statistics but very different visual patterns.  
D) ✗ Visualization remains necessary even when statistical measures are available.  

**Correct:** A, C
