## 11. Evaluation of Data Visualizations

## Questions

#### 1. Which of the following are important considerations when designing the text elements of a data visualization?  
A) Text orientation should always be horizontal except for line and axis labels.  
B) Data labels should be placed directly near the data points rather than in a separate legend.  
C) Titles should be short, descriptive sentences placed in the upper left corner.  
D) Axis labels can be smaller than 9-point font on paper if the graph is complex.  

#### 2. In the economic model of visualization value, which factors contribute to lowering the total cost of using a visualization method?  
A) Increasing the number of users (n).  
B) Minimizing the initial user cost (Cu).  
C) Increasing the number of exploration steps per session (k).  
D) Reducing the initial development cost (Ci).  

#### 3. Why might a technically advanced visualization method fail to be widely adopted in practice?  
A) The method always requires 3D effects, which users find confusing.  
B) The method may have a high initial user cost (Cu) that discourages adoption.  
C) The visualization might produce negative knowledge due to misleading artifacts.  
D) Existing simpler methods may provide sufficient knowledge gain (∆K).  

#### 4. Which statements about subjectivity in visualization are true?  
A) Sharing visualizations openly completely eliminates subjectivity.  
B) Visualization provides objective proof of data phenomena regardless of settings.  
C) Interaction and parameter tuning can increase subjectivity and exploration costs.  
D) Visualization outcomes depend on the user’s prior knowledge and perceptual skills.  

#### 5. When evaluating the use of color in data visualizations, which practices help ensure accessibility and clarity?  
A) Avoiding red-green and yellow-blue color combinations that touch each other.  
B) Using brand colors or intentional color schemes rather than default palettes.  
C) Using bright colors for all data points to maximize visibility.  
D) Ensuring the visualization remains interpretable when printed in black and white.  

#### 6. What are the potential drawbacks of excessive gridlines, borders, and tick marks in a graph?  
A) They always improve the accuracy of data reading.  
B) Borders help the graph stand out and should always be included.  
C) They can add clutter and distract from the data interpretation.  
D) Muted gray gridlines are preferable to black gridlines if gridlines are necessary.  

#### 7. According to the model, which of the following increase the *value* (profitability) of a visualization method?  
A) Large number of users (n).  
B) High initial development cost (Ci).  
C) High knowledge gain per session (W(∆K)).  
D) High frequency of use per user (m).  

#### 8. In what ways can interaction in visualization both help and hinder the user experience?  
A) Interaction allows users to explore large or complex data sets effectively.  
B) Good default settings can reduce the need for costly interaction.  
C) Interaction always reduces the time needed to understand the data.  
D) Excessive interaction options can increase subjectivity and user effort.  

#### 9. Which of the following best explain why presentation visualizations often have a higher impact than exploratory visualizations?  
A) Presentation visualizations usually have a larger audience with lower prior knowledge.  
B) Exploratory visualizations are always more aesthetically pleasing than presentation ones.  
C) Presentation visualizations have clear economic consequences, such as funding or sales.  
D) Exploratory visualizations rarely lead to actionable decisions.  

#### 10. Considering visualization as a scientific discipline, which challenges must be addressed to advance the field?  
A) Conducting empirical studies to evaluate effectiveness and user perception.  
B) Creating automated visualization generation tools that reduce user effort.  
C) Focusing solely on creating visually appealing images without empirical validation.  
D) Developing generic theories and taxonomies to classify methods and data types.

## Answers

#### 1. Which of the following are important considerations when designing the text elements of a data visualization?  
A) ✓ Text orientation should be horizontal for readability, except for some axis or line labels where deviation is acceptable.  
B) ✓ Data labels should be placed directly near data points to avoid distracting eye movement to legends.  
C) ✓ Titles should be short, descriptive sentences placed in the upper left corner. This aligns with reading patterns and helps quick comprehension.  
D) ✗ Axis labels should not be smaller than 9-point font on paper; smaller fonts reduce readability.  

**Correct:** A, B, C


#### 2. In the economic model of visualization value, which factors contribute to lowering the total cost of using a visualization method?  
A) ✗ Increasing number of users (n) increases total costs, though it may increase overall value.  
B) ✓ Minimizing initial user cost (Cu) reduces barriers for adoption and learning.  
C) ✗ Increasing exploration steps (k) raises user time and effort, increasing costs.  
D) ✓ Reducing initial development cost (Ci) lowers upfront investment, making the method more accessible.  

**Correct:** B, D


#### 3. Why might a technically advanced visualization method fail to be widely adopted in practice?  
A) ✗ 3D effects are not always required; many effective visualizations are 2D and avoid confusion.  
B) ✓ High initial user cost (Cu) discourages users from investing time and effort.  
C) ✓ Misleading artifacts can produce negative knowledge, reducing trust and usefulness.  
D) ✓ Existing simpler methods may provide sufficient knowledge gain, reducing incentive to switch.  

**Correct:** B, C, D


#### 4. Which statements about subjectivity in visualization are true?  
A) ✗ Sharing visualizations helps but does not eliminate inherent subjectivity in interpretation.  
B) ✗ Visualization does not provide objective proof; it is exploratory and influenced by settings.  
C) ✓ Interaction and parameter tuning increase subjectivity and exploration costs.  
D) ✓ Visualization outcomes depend on user knowledge and perception, making results subjective.  

**Correct:** C, D


#### 5. When evaluating the use of color in data visualizations, which practices help ensure accessibility and clarity?  
A) ✓ Avoiding problematic color combinations helps colorblind users interpret data correctly.  
B) ✓ Using intentional color schemes supports brand identity and thoughtful design.  
C) ✗ Using bright colors for all data points can overwhelm and reduce focus on key data.  
D) ✓ Ensuring legibility in black and white maintains interpretability when color is unavailable.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 6. What are the potential drawbacks of excessive gridlines, borders, and tick marks in a graph?  
A) ✗ Excessive lines do not always improve accuracy; they often cause noise.  
B) ✗ Borders often confine the graph unnecessarily and can reduce integration with surrounding content.  
C) ✓ They add clutter and distract from the data, reducing clarity.  
D) ✓ Muted gray gridlines are preferable to black if gridlines are necessary, to reduce visual weight.  

**Correct:** C, D


#### 7. According to the model, which of the following increase the *value* (profitability) of a visualization method?  
A) ✓ Large number of users (n) increases overall impact and value.  
B) ✗ High initial development cost (Ci) increases costs, reducing profitability.  
C) ✓ High knowledge gain per session (W(∆K)) increases the benefit derived from visualization.  
D) ✓ High frequency of use per user (m) increases total benefit from the method.  

**Correct:** A, C, D


#### 8. In what ways can interaction in visualization both help and hinder the user experience?  
A) ✓ Interaction enables exploration of complex or large data sets effectively.  
B) ✓ Good default settings reduce the need for costly interaction and help users get quick insights.  
C) ✗ Interaction does not always reduce time; it can increase effort and complexity.  
D) ✓ Excessive interaction options can overwhelm users and increase subjectivity.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 9. Which of the following best explain why presentation visualizations often have a higher impact than exploratory visualizations?  
A) ✓ Presentation visualizations reach larger audiences with less prior knowledge, increasing impact.  
B) ✗ Aesthetic appeal is not necessarily greater in exploratory visualizations; this is unrelated to impact.  
C) ✓ Presentation visualizations often have clear economic consequences like funding or sales.  
D) ✗ Exploratory visualizations can lead to actionable decisions; this is not a reason for lower impact.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 10. Considering visualization as a scientific discipline, which challenges must be addressed to advance the field?  
A) ✓ Empirical studies are needed to evaluate effectiveness and understand user perception.  
B) ✓ Automated visualization tools reduce user effort and improve accessibility.  
C) ✗ Focusing only on aesthetics without empirical validation limits scientific progress.  
D) ✓ Developing taxonomies and theories helps organize knowledge and guide research.  

**Correct:** A, B, D
