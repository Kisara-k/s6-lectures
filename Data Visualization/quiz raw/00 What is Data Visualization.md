## Answers

#### 1. What is a primary reason for involving a human in the visualization loop rather than relying solely on automatic solutions?  
A) ✓ Many analysis problems are ill-specified and the exact questions are not known in advance — Correct; humans help when questions are unclear.  
B) ✗ Humans can always process data faster than computers — Humans are not faster; speed is not the main reason.  
C) ✗ Automatic solutions are always less accurate than human judgment — Not necessarily true; accuracy depends on context.  
D) ✓ Visualization helps users verify and build trust in automatic solutions — Correct; humans verify and refine automatic outputs.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 2. Why is visualization particularly suitable for augmenting human capabilities?  
A) ✗ It replaces human cognition entirely with computational decision-making — Visualization augments, not replaces cognition.  
B) ✓ It leverages the human visual system’s high-bandwidth channel to the brain — Correct; vision is a high-bandwidth input channel.  
C) ✗ It allows humans to perceive data summaries without needing details — Details often matter; summaries alone are insufficient.  
D) ✓ It supports parallel and pre-attentive processing that humans excel at — Correct; vision processes information in parallel efficiently.  

**Correct:** B, D


#### 3. Which of the following are reasons why vision is preferred over other sensory modalities for data visualization?  
A) ✗ Sound provides a higher bandwidth and better overview than vision — Incorrect; sound is lower bandwidth and sequential.  
B) ✓ Touch/haptics have limited record/replay capacity and low bandwidth — Correct; touch is low bandwidth and less suitable.  
C) ✗ Taste and smell have viable record/replay devices suitable for visualization — Incorrect; no viable devices exist for these senses.  
D) ✓ Vision provides a high-bandwidth channel with simultaneous overview capability — Correct; vision supports overview and high bandwidth.  

**Correct:** B, D


#### 4. What is the role of external representation in visualization?  
A) ✗ To reduce the need for computational resources — Not the primary role; focus is on human cognition.  
B) ✗ To eliminate the need for human involvement in data analysis — Incorrect; humans remain central in the loop.  
C) ✗ To replace human perception with cognition — Incorrect; it replaces cognition with perception, not the other way.  
D) ✓ To replace cognition with perception — Correct; external representation offloads cognitive work to perception.  

**Correct:** D


#### 5. Why is it important to represent all the data rather than just summaries in visualization?  
A) ✗ Summaries always provide complete information about the data — Incorrect; summaries lose details.  
B) ✗ Representing all data wastes computational resources without benefit — Incorrect; representing all data is valuable despite costs.  
C) ✓ Details matter to confirm expected patterns and find unexpected ones — Correct; details reveal nuances and anomalies.  
D) ✓ Summaries can obscure the validity of statistical models — Correct; details help assess model validity.  

**Correct:** C, D


#### 6. Considering resource limitations in visualization design, which of the following are true?  
A) ✓ There is a tradeoff between clutter and wasted space in information density — Correct; balance is needed between clutter and whitespace.  
B) ✗ Computational limits such as system memory do not affect visualization design — Incorrect; computational limits are important constraints.  
C) ✓ Pixels are a precious and constrained resource in displays — Correct; pixels are limited and valuable.  
D) ✗ Human attention and memory are unlimited resources — Incorrect; human cognitive resources are limited.  

**Correct:** A, C


#### 7. What is the purpose of analyzing visualization designs systematically?  
A) ✓ To impose structure on a huge design space and scaffold thinking — Correct; analysis helps organize design decisions.  
B) ✗ To avoid designing new visualizations by only using existing ones — Incorrect; analysis supports both understanding and new design.  
C) ✗ To randomly select design choices without structure — Incorrect; analysis imposes structure, not randomness.  
D) ✓ To identify ineffective possibilities for particular task/data combinations — Correct; analysis helps discard poor options.  

**Correct:** A, D


#### 8. Which of the following statements about the human visual system in visualization are correct?  
A) ✓ It provides a subjective experience of seeing everything simultaneously — Correct; users feel they see the whole scene at once.  
B) ✓ It supports significant parallel and pre-attentive processing — Correct; vision processes many elements simultaneously.  
C) ✗ It processes information sequentially, limiting overview capabilities — Incorrect; vision processes in parallel, enabling overview.  
D) ✗ It is less effective than sound for providing an overview of data — Incorrect; vision is more effective for overview.  

**Correct:** A, B


#### 9. In what scenarios is visualization particularly useful?  
A) ✓ For exploratory analysis of scientific data over the long term — Correct; visualization supports exploration.  
B) ✓ For presentation of known results to an audience — Correct; visualization aids communication.  
C) ✗ When a fully automatic and trusted solution exists — Incorrect; visualization is less needed if automatic solutions are trusted.  
D) ✓ To help developers refine parameters of automatic solutions — Correct; visualization helps refine and tune models.  

**Correct:** A, B, D


#### 10. What challenges must visualization designers balance when encoding information visually?  
A) ✓ Balancing computational limits, display constraints, and human factors — Correct; all three resource types must be balanced.  
B) ✗ Using as many pixels as possible regardless of whitespace or clutter — Incorrect; indiscriminate pixel use leads to clutter or wasted space.  
C) ✗ Ignoring human cognitive limits to focus on computational efficiency — Incorrect; human limits are critical considerations.  
D) ✓ Maximizing information density without causing clutter — Correct; must find a balance between dense and sparse.  

**Correct:** A, D
